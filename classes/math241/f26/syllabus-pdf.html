Description

A first course in mathematical probability that covers topics including combinatorics, discrete and continuous random variables, distribution functions, joint distributions, independence, properties of expectations, and basic limit theorems. No prior background in statistics or probability is expected.

Course Prerequisite: MTH 126-127 (i.e. Calculus II), or equivalent.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to do the following:

  • Conceptualize various foundational ideas in probability theory, including the axioms of probability, combinatorial probability, conditional probability, and independence.
  • Work with discrete and continuous probability spaces through the use of random variables, with their associated distribution functions and moments, recognizing patterns and similarities between different families of probability distributions.
  • Apply tail inequalities, the law of large numbers, and the central limit theorem to study and approximate distribution properties.
  • Use technical tools appropriately, such as R, RStudio, and generative AI, to simulate concepts and problems in probability theory, as well as perform meaningful calculations.
  • Communicate statistical results and real-world impacts to various stakeholders by written, visual, and verbal means.

A standard grading system will be used here. Cumulative numerical averages of 90-100 are guaranteed at least an A-, 80-89 at least a B-, and 70-79 at least a C-.

Engagement (5%)

Success in this course will involve strong engagement with course material, both inside and outside the classroom. This includes the assignment of readings and materials, interacting with content and peers in the classroom, and bringing concerns and questions to the instructor when needed.

Readings and materials will be assigned outside of class for you to prepare for the following class. This will involve chapters from the textbook, external readings, videos, etc. We will discuss these materials in-class, so be prepared to discuss what you learned and saw.

Attendance is strongly expected and will be incorporated into your engagement grade. Additionally, when you are in class, you are expected to be actively engaged. Below is a rubric for how engagement will be scored:

You are strongly recommended to bring your laptop to all lectures. We will be using tools such as R and RStudio to work through examples and submit assignments during class.

Checkpoints (20% combined)

Some Thursday classes will be dedicated to checkpoints, which are designed to track your progress with concepts, definitions, and applications in this course. Either by yourself or in a group, 2-3 problems will be solved using your notes and R. They will be completed in-class and turned in by the end of the period. Six checkpoints will be administered throughout the semester.

  • Checkpoints will be graded based on correctness.
  • Your lowest checkpoint will be dropped from your final grade.

Homework (20% total)

Homework will be assigned on almost a weekly basis, with these problem sets generally due on Fridays by 11:59pm local time. Problem sets consists of calculations, short answers, and inference, which will demonstrate your conceptual understanding and allow you to practice the methodological components. Homework will be submitted as PDFs on Gradescope and can be completed through a typesetting program (e.g. LaTeX) or by-hand.

  • Homework will be graded based on correctness and completion.
  • Your lowest homework assignment will be dropped from your final grade.

Project (15%)

A semester-long project will be assigned sometime around weeks 3-5 and due by the end of the semester, with checkpoints assigned along the way. The project is designed to test your ability to successfully complete course objectives as described earlier.

More details to come in later weeks.

Policies

Preparation and Attendance

Expect to spend an average of 10 hours per week on this course, with 2.5 hours spent in-class and 7.5 hours outside of class. Outside of class, you are expected to read through lecture notes, readings, and examples and work on problems (via homework, lab assignments, studying, practice) in preparation for class and beyond. In-class, you are expected to take part in class discussions, exercises, activities, and lectures.

As mentioned earlier, attendance is expected and will be incorporated into your engagement grade. If you are unable to attend class resulting from family/personal difficulties, sickness, religious observations, or school/career-related activities, please let me know as soon as possible.

Do make every effort to arrive to class on time. If you happen/plan to arrive more than 10 minutes late, please inform me ahead of time. Any unexplained tardiness will result in a marked absence. If you must miss a class entirely, you should contact a peer to discuss what was missed.

Collaboration and Class Norms

Inside the classroom, remember that this is a learning space for everyone, and as such, keep these ideals in mind:

  • Respect others and yourself as you participate within the class environment.
  • Embrace challenge gracefully. Ask questions as they come up.
  • Participate to the best of your ability.

Collaboration will occur frequently within the classroom, and naturally outside of class. You are welcome to study and work together as you complete assignments and prepare for exams. However, any written work or code should be your own product (unless otherwise stated). If any code or work is based on those seen from another source (such as StackExchange), please cite the source.

Note: Generative AI is not recommended for doing any part of any assignment (unless otherwise stated), and will not be allowed in any capacity on in-class assignments and exams. See the Generative AI policy for more details.

Extensions and Late Work

You will be granted 3 free late days to use for homework assignments throughout the semester, with a maximum of one day used on each assignment. Beyond this, late assignments will not be accepted without an accommodation from a class dean.

Academic Honesty

As a student at Vassar College, the college expects all students to be honest and committed to the principles of academic and intellectual integrity in preparation and submission of all course work and examinations. Any cases of academic dishonesty or plagiarism will be reported and may result in a score of 0 for ALL involved persons on the assignment in question. Examples of these behaviors include:

  • Submitting work completed by another student as your own.
  • Copying and pasting words from sources without quoting and citing the author.
  • Paraphrasing material from another source without citing the author.
  • Failing to cite your sources correctly.
  • Falsifying or misrepresenting information in submitted work.
  • Paying another student or service to complete assignments for you.
  • Submitting work generated by artificially intelligent tools such as ChatGPT without permission or instruction to do so.

You are encouraged to discuss course material, including assignments, with your classmates. All work you turn in, however, must be your own. This includes both writing and code.

Generative AI

Generative AI is an incredible tool that can be used to facilitate tasks both small and large; however, it should not be a substitute for your own learning of material and concepts in this course. Imagine a generative AI model as another person: if that model is doing something that wouldn’t be permissible if they were a human, it probably will not fly here.

Formalizing the analogy, you are open to use generative AI on the following items:

  • Formatting/proofreading of outside assignments (excluding the project)
  • Creation of study aids for preparation purposes
  • Generation of practice problems aside from the assignments

On the flip side, any use of generative AI (unless otherwise stated) is prohibited on all in-class assignments and exams. The policy is meant to encourage use for your own learning outside the classroom, while adhering to your own cognitive processes within the classroom for improved learning and comprehension.

Prohibited forms of generative AI usage include but are not limited to:

  • Drafting responses to written prompts on any assignment.
  • Composing code and comments.
  • Answering lab, quiz, or exam questions.
  • Conducting analysis of any plots, methods, and results for any assignment.

Any unauthorized use of generative artificial intelligence in this course will be considered a case of academic dishonesty/plagiarism and will be reported.
